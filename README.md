# Education, Social Origin and Occupational Status in China

This project examines where urban-rural inequality appears among higher-education graduates in China. Is the divide more visible in the qualifications people obtain, or in their occupational positions after education and family background are taken into account?

The submitted analysis uses the 2017 China General Social Survey (CGSS). The final sample contains 1,646 graduates aged 25 to 60 with valid current or recent non-farm occupation data. Occupational status is measured with the International Socio-Economic Index of Occupational Status (ISEI-08), and the original analysis uses a sequence of OLS models.

Education level is strongly associated with occupational position. In the adjusted model, bachelor graduates score about 6.2 ISEI points above junior-college graduates, while the postgraduate difference is about 15.9 points. Rural-origin graduates are also more concentrated in the lower tiers of higher education.

One result was less expected. After education, parental education, age and gender are controlled, rural origin has a positive coefficient of 3.02 points (SE = 0.83, p < .001). The direction runs against the expected rural penalty. I do not take it as evidence that rural origin itself provides an occupational advantage. The analysis only includes people who reached higher education, and stronger selection into this group among rural-origin respondents is one possible explanation. The education-by-origin interactions also provide no evidence of a negative within-tier rural penalty.

Younger cohorts tend to have lower occupational status in the cohort specification. This finding is harder to interpret. Everyone is observed in 2017, so birth cohort and age cannot be separated in this dataset. The result therefore cannot distinguish cohort change in educational returns from ordinary career progression.

The project began as my undergraduate sociology dissertation at the University of Edinburgh. After submission, I returned to the original data and SPSS files and rebuilt the analysis in R. The submitted workflow is reproduced first. Later measurement and modelling changes are kept separate.

## What this repository contains

Stages 01 to 04 reconstruct the submitted SPSS analysis from the archived CGSS 2017 data and final syntax. They rebuild the sample, reproduce the ISEI scores generated by the submitted workflow, and recover Models 1 to 7.

Validation is tied directly to the final SPSS output. Sample counts and other structural quantities are checked exactly. Coefficients, conventional standard errors, model fit statistics and VIFs are checked at the precision reported by SPSS. The main cross-tabulations and grouped ISEI means are reproduced as well.

Estimate-by-estimate comparison uncovered two minor reporting discrepancies in the submitted dissertation. Neither changed the fitted models or substantive conclusions. The details are recorded in [`docs/replication_audit.md`](docs/replication_audit.md).

The R audit also adds two joint tests of the interaction blocks. These were not reported in the submitted dissertation, so they are identified as new diagnostic checks rather than reproduced statistics.

Stages 01 to 04 now form the fixed reproduction baseline. They are not altered when later revisions are introduced.

## Data and measures

The project uses the 2017 wave of the China General Social Survey.

The reproduction sample includes respondents aged 25 to 60 who completed junior college or above and had a valid current or recent non-farm occupation.

Rural origin is measured primarily from hukou status at age 14. Rural village and township hukou are coded as rural origin. County-seat, suburban and city hukou are grouped as urban origin. Residence at age 14 provides an alternative origin measure in Model 7.

Parental education is based on the highest recorded education of either parent, converted into years of schooling. The reproduction retains the coding used in the submitted dissertation. One ambiguous parental-education category is reconsidered later in the revised analysis.

The submitted models use OLS with conventional standard errors. Stages 01 to 04 keep that specification because their purpose is to reproduce the analysis that was actually submitted.

The respondent-level CGSS data are not redistributed here. CGSS is run by the National Survey Research Center at Renmin University of China, and current data access is provided through Renmin University's social science data infrastructure, including the Social Science Data Resource Platform (CSSD).

To run the reproduction, place a local copy of the required SAV file at:

```text

```

## Running the reproduction

The verified reproduction has been run under R 4.4.3.

The `haven` package is used to read the SPSS file. The remaining model estimation and validation code relies mainly on base R. Exact environment information from the verified run is stored in [`docs/session_info.txt`](docs/session_info.txt).

Open the RStudio project:

```text
education-stratification-china.Rproj
```

Place the CGSS SAV file in `data/raw/`, then run:

```r
source("R/04_original_models.R")
```

Stage 04 calls Stages 01 to 03, so the analysis is rebuilt from the source SAV file rather than loaded from a saved analytic dataset.

A successful run ends with:

```text
04_original_models.R: ALL CHECKS PASSED
```

Aggregate results are written to:

```text
output/original_replication/
```

No respondent-level CGSS records are written to that folder.

## Current status

The reproduction of the submitted SPSS analysis is complete.

The next technical step is an audit of the ISCO-08 to ISEI-08 conversion used in the submitted workflow. A documented replacement mapping will be compared with the original mapping before the substantive models are rerun. The comparison will show where occupational scores change and whether those changes affect the analytic sample or the results.

Other revisions, including the treatment of the ambiguous parental-education category and revised statistical inference, will be handled in the same later stage.

The reproduced analysis remains unchanged. New measurement and modelling decisions are kept in separate scripts so their effects can be compared with the submitted workflow.
