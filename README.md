# Education, Social Origin and Occupational Status in China

This repository contains the R reproduction of my undergraduate sociology dissertation at the University of Edinburgh. The original analysis was carried out in SPSS with the 2017 China General Social Survey (CGSS). I later returned to the archived data, syntax and output and rebuilt the analysis in R from the source SAV file rather than from a saved analytic sample.

The dissertation asks where urban-rural inequality appears among people who reached higher education in China: in the qualifications they obtain, in occupational position after graduation, or both. Occupational position is measured with ISEI-08. The submitted analysis uses seven unweighted OLS models and a final analytic sample of 1,646 respondents.

Respondent-level CGSS data are not redistributed here.

## Sample

The analysis starts with 12,582 CGSS respondents. The sample is then restricted to people aged 25–60 who reported junior college or above and had a valid current or most recent non-farm occupation.

```text
12,582  CGSS 2017 respondents
 2,470  junior college or above
 1,766  aged 25–60
 1,674  valid current or most recent non-farm occupation
 1,646  final analytic sample
```

The sample is rebuilt directly from the survey items. The old diagnostic sample flag stored in the archived working file is not used to define the 1,646 cases.

Education is grouped into junior college, bachelor and postgraduate levels, with junior college as the reference category. The main origin measure comes from A27H, which records the type of place where the respondent's hukou was registered at age 14. Codes 1–2 are grouped as rural origin and codes 3–5 as urban origin. Model 7 repeats the main specification with A27F, place of residence at age 14.

Parental education is the higher available education level of either parent after conversion to approximate years of schooling. Occupational status uses the ISEI-08 scores generated by the submitted SPSS workflow and reconstructed in Stage 03.

## Results reproduced

Education is strongly associated with occupational position. In Model 3, the bachelor coefficient is 6.205 ISEI points and the postgraduate coefficient is 15.899, relative to junior college.

The rural-origin coefficient in the same model is 3.025 (SE = 0.833, p < .001). I do not read the positive sign as a causal rural advantage. The analysis conditions on having reached higher education, so selection into the higher-education sample remains part of the interpretation.

The education-by-origin interaction block is not jointly significant. The cross-tabulation by education and origin nevertheless shows clear sorting across qualification levels: rural-origin respondents are more concentrated in junior college and less concentrated in the higher tiers.

Later-born cohorts have lower occupational-status estimates in the cohort specification. Since everyone is observed in 2017, the design cannot cleanly separate cohort differences from career-stage differences. Model 6 adds education-by-cohort interactions, but the oldest-cohort postgraduate cell contains only two respondents and the resulting VIFs are very large, so I treat that model as exploratory.

## Reproduction checks

Stages 01–04 reproduce the submitted SPSS analysis.

The checks are tied to the archived final output rather than to a second saved analytic dataset. Sample counts, residual degrees of freedom and table cell counts are checked exactly. Coefficients and classical standard errors are compared with the precision printed by SPSS, along with the available fit statistics and VIF benchmarks.

The R scripts reproduce:

- the sample-construction sequence;
- the education, origin, cohort, parental-education and occupation variables;
- the ISEI-08 scores used in the submitted workflow;
- Models 1–7;
- Table 6, education by urban-rural origin;
- Table 7, mean ISEI by birth cohort and education level;
- Appendix Table A1, education by birth cohort.

I also calculated two joint tests from the reproduced models:

```text
Education × rural origin   F(2, 1637) = 1.501, p = .223
Education × cohort         F(6, 1631) = 1.164, p = .323
```

These two tests were calculated in R after the original analysis; they are not substituted for statistics reported in the dissertation.

## Running it

The verified reproduction was run under R 4.4.3. The core workflow uses `haven` to read the SPSS file; the model estimation and most checks use base R.

```r
install.packages("haven")
```

Place the archived SAV file at:

```text
data/raw/CGSS2017.sav
```

Then open `education-stratification-china.Rproj` and run:

```r
source("R/04_original_models.R")
```

Stage 04 sources Stages 03, 02 and 01, so this rebuilds the analysis from the SAV file. A successful run ends with:

```text
04_original_models.R: ALL CHECKS PASSED
```

Aggregate outputs are written to `output/original_replication/`. No respondent-level analytic dataset is written by these scripts.

## Data access

CGSS is conducted by the National Survey Research Center at Renmin University of China. The respondent-level CGSS 2017 data are not included in this repository.

The archived working SAV used for the reproduction has the following fingerprint:

```text
Cases       12,582

File size   26,231,277 bytes
MD5         4933ebaa85a5d2e38c1a15aa5ae6c04e
```

It contains 12 diagnostic fields created during the original workflow. Stage 01 removes those fields before analysis and works with the remaining 786 survey variables.

The raw-data directory is excluded from Git. Environment details for the verified run are in `docs/session_info.txt`.
